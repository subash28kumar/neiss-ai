# Labelling definitions: regular e-bike, likely e-moto, or unclear (v2, fixed before labelling)

Every story was coded by hospital staff as an e-bike injury. Decide what kind of vehicle the
rider was on. Read only the part before the diagnosis ("DX"): phrases after DX such as
"FALL FROM MOTORIZED BIKE" or "DRIVER OF DIRT BIKE OR MOTOR/CROSS BIKE INJURED" are coding
categories, not a description of the vehicle.

**Likely e-moto** — choose this when the story:
- uses e-moto wording: electric motorcycle, electric dirt bike, e-moto, gas- or engine-powered
  bike, a bike with a clutch or gears like a motorcycle, or a brand such as Sur-Ron or Talaria
  (brands are often blacked out as "***"); or
- gives the rider's own speed above 28 mph (the top assisted speed of a legal Class 3 e-bike),
  when the vehicle is called an e-bike or electric bike/bicycle and the rider was not going downhill.

**Unclear** — choose this when:
- the vehicle is called a "motorized bike", "motorized bicycle", "motor bike" or "motorbike"
  (at any speed, with or without a speed);
- the wording could mean either ("dirt bike or e-bike", "e-bike vs dirt bike");
- a speed range crosses 28 mph ("25-30 mph");
- the speed above 28 mph was going downhill;
- the speed above 28 mph is someone else's guess or is disputed (for example "bystanders thought
  he was going 30 mph"); or
- the story says "fast", "high speed" or "high rate of speed" with no number.

**Regular e-bike** — everything else. Ignore speeds that belong to another vehicle or another
rider ("struck by a car going 30 mph"). A speed of 28 mph or less is a regular e-bike unless
other wording above applies. Patient's own estimates ("about 30 mph", "approximately 35 mph")
count as the rider's speed.
