E-bike injuries in Kentucky

In 2025, about 1,400 people in Kentucky went to an emergency room after an e-bike injury (likely range 940 to 1,800). That is about 4 a day, or 1 in 3,400 residents, and about 12 times the 2020 level.

These injury numbers are estimates: national NEISS emergency department data, applied to Kentucky's population in each age group. NEISS does not record the patient's state, so no one can count them directly from it. The likely range means the true number is very likely (95%) inside it.

Public health and transportation

  • Hospital stays: about 220 of the 2025 e-bike visits likely ended in a hospital stay (national hospital-stay shares by age applied to Kentucky).
  • Who: the most visits were among people aged 25-44.
  • Road deaths (real count): in 2024, 11 bicyclists, including e-bike riders, were killed in traffic crashes with motor vehicles in Kentucky: 1.6% of all traffic deaths, 0.24 per 100,000 people, lower than the US rate of 0.32. Source: NHTSA FARS.
  • Biking to work (real survey): about 6,000 people in Kentucky usually bike to work (0.29% of workers; US 0.47%), Census ACS 2025. More riding usually means more bike injuries.
  • Medical bills (estimate): e-bike injuries in Kentucky in 2025 likely cost about $11 million to $25 million in medical care (2025 dollars). The low number is if every injury cost like a fall with no car; the high number is if every injury cost like a crash with a car. Source: CDC study.
