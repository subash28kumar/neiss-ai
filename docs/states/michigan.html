E-bike injuries in Michigan

If people in Michigan were hurt at the national rate for their age group, about 3,000 would have gone to an emergency room after an e-bike injury in 2025 (range 2,200 to 3,800, from the national estimate). That is about 8 a day, or 1 in 3,400 residents, and about 13 times the 2020 level.

These are expected numbers, not counts. NEISS does not record the patient's state, so no one can count Michigan's visits from it. NEISS-AI takes the national rate for each age group and applies it to Michigan's population. The real number can be higher or lower, for example where more or fewer people ride. The range shows only the uncertainty in the national number, not how Michigan differs from other states.

How the number is worked out

Michigan visits = add up, for each age group: national visits ÷ US population × Michigan population

blue = whole US (NEISS and Census)   green = Michigan  ·  See the worked example

What this number is for
  • Good for: a rough local figure for grant applications, reports and safety plans, and a benchmark to check your own hospital counts against.
  • Not for: ranking states or judging how safe Michigan is. Every state gets the same national rate for each age group, so differences between states come only from population size and age mix, not from local riding or road safety.

Public health and transportation

  • Hospital stays: about 480 of the 2025 e-bike visits likely ended in a hospital stay (national hospital-stay shares by age applied to Michigan).
  • Who: the most visits were among people aged 25-44.
  • Road deaths (real count): in 2024, 29 bicyclists, including e-bike riders, were killed in traffic crashes with motor vehicles in Michigan: 2.6% of all traffic deaths, 0.29 per 100,000 people, close to the US rate of 0.32. Source: NHTSA FARS.
  • Biking to work (real survey): about 16,300 people in Michigan usually bike to work (0.34% of workers; US 0.47%), Census ACS 2025. More riding usually means more bike injuries.
  • Medical bills (estimate): e-bike injuries in Michigan in 2025 likely cost about $24 million to $55 million in medical care (2025 dollars). The low number is if every injury cost like a fall with no car; the high number is if every injury cost like a crash with a car. Source: CDC study.
