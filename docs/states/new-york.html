E-bike injuries in New York

If people in New York were hurt at the national rate for their age group, about 5,900 would have gone to an emergency room after an e-bike injury in 2025 (range 4,000 to 7,800, from the national estimate). That is about 16 a day, or 1 in 3,400 residents, and about 12 times the 2020 level.

These are expected numbers, not counts. NEISS does not record the patient's state, so no one can count New York's visits from it. NEISS-AI takes the national rate for each age group and applies it to New York's population. The real number can be higher or lower, for example where more or fewer people ride. The range shows only the uncertainty in the national number, not how New York differs from other states.

Public health and transportation

  • Hospital stays: about 950 of the 2025 e-bike visits likely ended in a hospital stay (national hospital-stay shares by age applied to New York).
  • Who: the most visits were among people aged 25-44.
  • Road deaths (real count): in 2024, 47 bicyclists, including e-bike riders, were killed in traffic crashes with motor vehicles in New York: 4.3% of all traffic deaths, 0.24 per 100,000 people, lower than the US rate of 0.32. Source: NHTSA FARS.
  • Biking to work (real survey): about 87,100 people in New York usually bike to work (0.90% of workers; US 0.47%), Census ACS 2025. More riding usually means more bike injuries.
  • Medical bills (estimate): e-bike injuries in New York in 2025 likely cost about $47 million to $109 million in medical care (2025 dollars). The low number is if every injury cost like a fall with no car; the high number is if every injury cost like a crash with a car. Source: CDC study.
