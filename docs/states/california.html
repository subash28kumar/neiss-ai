E-bike injuries in California

In 2025, about 11,800 people in California went to an emergency room after an e-bike injury (likely range 8,100 to 15,600). That is about 32 a day, or 1 in 3,300 residents, and about 12 times the 2020 level.

These injury numbers are estimates: national NEISS emergency department data, applied to California's population in each age group. NEISS does not record the patient's state, so no one can count them directly from it. The likely range means the true number is very likely (95%) inside it.

Public health and transportation

  • Hospital stays: about 1,900 of the 2025 e-bike visits likely ended in a hospital stay (national hospital-stay shares by age applied to California).
  • Who: the most visits were among people aged 25-44.
  • Road deaths (real count): in 2024, 163 bicyclists, including e-bike riders, were killed in traffic crashes with motor vehicles in California: 4.2% of all traffic deaths, 0.41 per 100,000 people, higher than the US rate of 0.32. Source: NHTSA FARS.
  • Biking to work (real survey): about 134,100 people in California usually bike to work (0.72% of workers; US 0.47%), Census ACS 2025. More riding usually means more bike injuries.
  • Medical bills (estimate): e-bike injuries in California in 2025 likely cost about $94 million to $218 million in medical care (2025 dollars). The low number is if every injury cost like a fall with no car; the high number is if every injury cost like a crash with a car. Source: CDC study.
