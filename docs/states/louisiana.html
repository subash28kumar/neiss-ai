E-bike injuries in Louisiana

If people in Louisiana were hurt at the national rate for their age group, about 1,400 would have gone to an emergency room after an e-bike injury in 2025 (range 990 to 1,800, from the national estimate). That is about 4 a day, or 1 in 3,400 residents, and about 12 times the 2020 level.

These are expected numbers, not counts. NEISS does not record the patient's state, so no one can count Louisiana's visits from it. NEISS-AI takes the national rate for each age group and applies it to Louisiana's population. The real number can be higher or lower, for example where more or fewer people ride. The range shows only the uncertainty in the national number, not how Louisiana differs from other states.

Public health and transportation

  • Hospital stays: about 220 of the 2025 e-bike visits likely ended in a hospital stay (national hospital-stay shares by age applied to Louisiana).
  • Who: the most visits were among people aged 25-44.
  • Road deaths (real count): in 2024, 44 bicyclists, including e-bike riders, were killed in traffic crashes with motor vehicles in Louisiana: 5.9% of all traffic deaths, 0.96 per 100,000 people, higher than the US rate of 0.32. Source: NHTSA FARS.
  • Biking to work (real survey): about 5,900 people in Louisiana usually bike to work (0.28% of workers; US 0.47%), Census ACS 2025. More riding usually means more bike injuries.
  • Medical bills (estimate): e-bike injuries in Louisiana in 2025 likely cost about $11 million to $25 million in medical care (2025 dollars). The low number is if every injury cost like a fall with no car; the high number is if every injury cost like a crash with a car. Source: CDC study.
