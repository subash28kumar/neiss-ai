How NEISS-AI works

  1. Read every NEISS record. Each record is one emergency department visit, with product codes, the patient's age and a short injury story written by hospital staff.
  2. Label the device. Product codes and whole-word keywords in the story identify e-bikes, e-scooters, bicycles and similar devices. Before 2024 e-bikes shared code 3215 with mopeds; when the story is unclear, a text classifier trained on 2024–2025 records (where the codes are separate) decides. Cross-validated accuracy: AUC 0.995.
  3. Estimate national totals. NEISS is a stratified sample of hospitals, so each estimate uses survey weights and Taylor-linearized 95% confidence intervals, and flags unstable numbers using CPSC's rule. E-bike estimates for 2022–2024 match CPSC's published figures within 4%.

Use it yourself

Free and open source (MIT licence). Download the NEISS annual files from CPSC into a folder called data, then:

pip install git+https://github.com/subash28kumar/neiss-ai
neissai micromobility --years 2019-2025 --data-dir data --out-dir results
neissai classify "14YOM FELL OFF HIS ELECTRIC BIKE GOING 20MPH"

Results are saved as CSV files: national estimates by year, device and age group, plus hospitalisation, head-injury, motor-vehicle and helmet shares.

Limits: NEISS counts emergency visits, not riders or miles, so these are counts, not risk rates. Helmet use is only what the story mentions. Early e-bike estimates (2019–2021) rest on small samples.
